How to Increase Energy Naturally: 7 Evidence-Informed Daily Habits

Persistent low energy is rarely solved by another cup of coffee. Explore seven evidence-informed daily habits that support sustainable, everyday wellbeing.

Energy & Performance • ⏱ 7 min read • Published 11 Aug 2026

Feeling tired occasionally is normal. But when low energy becomes part of everyday life, it can affect work, exercise, concentration and quality of life. The solution isn't always another cup of coffee or another supplement. Often, the biggest improvements come from the basics: sleep, food, movement, hydration and stress management. Here are seven evidence-informed habits that can support everyday energy.

1. Prioritize Consistent Sleep

Sleep is one of the most important foundations of wellbeing. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine and Sleep Research Society recommend that adults regularly get at least seven hours of sleep, while other consensus recommendations commonly place healthy adult sleep needs around seven to nine hours depending on the individual.

Try to:

  • Maintain a consistent sleep schedule
  • Create a relaxing bedtime routine
  • Keep your sleeping environment comfortable
  • Reduce unnecessary late-night screen exposure
  • Avoid making sleep an afterthought

If persistent tiredness continues despite adequate sleep, consider discussing it with a healthcare professional.

2. Eat Balanced Meals

Your body needs energy from food. A balanced eating pattern can include protein, whole grains or other carbohydrate sources, vegetables and fruit, healthy fats, and adequate fluids. Instead of relying on highly processed snacks for quick energy, build meals that provide a mixture of nutrients.

3. Stay Hydrated

Hydration is easy to overlook during a busy day. Keep water accessible, especially when working, travelling or exercising. Fluid requirements vary according to factors including activity, environment, diet and individual circumstances. Instead of waiting until you feel extremely thirsty, make regular hydration part of your daily routine.

4. Move More During the Day

Exercise is not only about body composition. Regular physical activity supports overall physical and mental wellbeing.

WHO guidance

WHO recommends that adults accumulate 150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week, or an equivalent amount of vigorous activity, along with muscle-strengthening activities on at least two days each week.

You don't need to start with an extreme workout. Try brisk walking, cycling, swimming, strength training, yoga, dancing or recreational sports. Start where you are and build gradually.

5. Break Up Long Periods of Sitting

Many modern jobs involve hours at a desk. Instead of remaining seated continuously: stand up, walk for a few minutes, stretch, take the stairs, or walk while taking a phone call. Small amounts of movement can help reduce sedentary time. WHO recommends limiting sedentary behaviour and replacing some sedentary time with physical activity of any intensity.

6. Manage Stress

Energy isn't only physical. Mental overload can leave you feeling exhausted even after a relatively inactive day. Consider short breaks during work, time outdoors, breathing exercises, hobbies, social connection, reducing unnecessary notifications and setting reasonable work boundaries.

Wellness is not about maximizing productivity every minute. Recovery matters too.

7. Consider Functional Ingredients Carefully

Once the foundations are in place, some people explore functional foods and supplements. Cordyceps is one ingredient that has been investigated in relation to exercise and physical performance. The evidence is mixed: some human trials have reported improvements in selected exercise measures, while other trials have found no meaningful performance benefit.

Language matters

Vanya Global avoids saying "Cordyceps gives everyone more energy." A better statement is: Cordyceps is a functional ingredient being studied for exercise and physical performance.

When Tiredness Needs More Attention

Lifestyle changes can support normal wellbeing, but persistent fatigue shouldn't automatically be treated as a nutrition problem. Talk with a healthcare professional if tiredness persists for an extended period, interferes significantly with daily activities, is unexplained, occurs with other concerning symptoms, or does not improve despite adequate rest. Fatigue can have many possible causes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I increase energy naturally?
Start with adequate sleep, balanced nutrition, hydration, regular physical activity and stress management.
What is the best natural energy booster?
There is no single best natural energy booster. Sleep, nutrition, physical activity and hydration form the foundation.
Does exercise increase energy?
Regular physical activity supports overall health and wellbeing and can be part of a healthy strategy for maintaining energy.
Can Cordyceps improve energy?
Cordyceps is researched for exercise and physical performance, but human evidence is mixed and it should not be presented as a guaranteed energy booster.
When should I see a doctor about tiredness?
Persistent or unexplained fatigue should be discussed with a qualified healthcare professional.

Final Takeaway

You don't need to completely redesign your life to support better energy. Start with seven fundamentals: sleep, nutrition, hydration, movement, less sitting, stress management and thoughtful supplementation.

The goal isn't temporary stimulation. It's sustainable everyday wellbeing.
